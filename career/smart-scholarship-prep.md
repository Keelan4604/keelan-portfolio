## 1. Scholarship Overview & Benefits

The Science, Mathematics And Research for Transformation (SMART) Scholarship-for-Service Program is funded by the DoD and administered by Scholarship America. It is one of the most valuable STEM scholarships in the country.

### What You Get

| Benefit | Amount |
|---|---|
| **Tuition** | Full tuition at any regionally accredited U.S. university |
| **Stipend (MS level)** | ~$33,000 - $41,000/year (varies; recent data says $30K-$46K range by degree) |
| **Book allowance** | $1,000/academic year |
| **Health insurance** | Up to $1,200 - $2,500/year (sources vary; likely increased recently) |
| **Summer internship pay** | ~$1,000/week on top of monthly stipend |
| **Mentorship** | Assigned DoD mentor at sponsoring facility |
| **Guaranteed employment** | Civilian DoD job immediately after graduation |

**For Keelan's situation (2-year MS):** This would cover UMich's ~$61K/year out-of-state tuition, plus $33K-$41K stipend, plus summer internship income. Total value over 2 years: roughly $180K-$210K+.

### Service Obligation

- **1:1 ratio** -- for each year of scholarship funding, you owe one year of civilian DoD employment after graduation
- 2-year MS = 2 years of post-graduation DoD civilian work
- Employment starts immediately after degree completion
- MS graduates typically start at **GS-9** pay grade (~$60K-$70K+ depending on locality)
- Automatic progression to GS-11, GS-12 over successive years
- Federal benefits: TSP (401k) with 5% match, pension, healthcare, student loan repayment eligibility

### Eligibility Requirements

- U.S. citizen (or citizen of Australia, Canada, New Zealand, UK)
- Minimum 3.0 GPA (competitive average is 3.70+)
- Enrolled full-time at a regionally accredited U.S. institution
- Able to obtain and hold a security clearance
- Willing to complete summer internships at a DoD facility
- Willing to accept post-graduation DoD civilian employment

## 3. Application Process & Requirements

### Timeline

- **Application opens:** August 1, 2026
- **Deadline:** First Friday of December 2026 (likely December 4, 2026), 5:00 PM EST
- **All supporting materials** (references, GRE scores) must be received by deadline
- **Semifinalist notifications:** February 2027
- **Facility interviews:** February-March 2027
- **Award notifications:** April 2027
- **Accept/decline window:** 10 days after notification

### Required Materials

1. **Online application** (through smartscholarship.org portal)
2. **Personal statement** (3,500 - 7,000 characters including spaces)
3. **Resume/CV**
4. **Official transcripts** from all institutions attended
5. **GRE General Test scores** (required for all graduate applicants; subject tests NOT required)
6. **2-3 reference letters** (minimum 2, maximum 3)
7. **Facility preferences** (rank your preferred DoD sponsoring facilities)
8. **Proposed award details** (how many years of funding you're requesting)

### GRE Details

- Graduate applicants MUST have taken the GRE General Test
- Scores must be reportable by ETS by the December deadline
- **Take the GRE by November 2026 at the latest** to ensure scores arrive in time
- GMAT/MCAT/LSAT cannot be substituted
- No published minimum score, but it is "heavily weighted"

## 4. Personal Statement Strategy

### Structure (based on winning application advice from SMART forum panelists)

The personal statement should have 5 clear sections:

#### Paragraph 1: Personal Background & Motivation
- Why aerospace engineering? What drives you?
- Brief origin story connecting to defense/aerospace interest
- Keep it authentic and concise

#### Paragraph 2: Research Interests & Specific Project Ideas
- NOT vague ("I want to study aerospace"). SPECIFIC research hypotheses and aims.
- Keelan's angle: UAV aeroacoustics, propeller optimization for unmanned systems, CFD-driven design of next-gen aerial vehicles
- Mention planned UMich coursework/research in CFD, FEA, flight dynamics
- Show you have an actual research direction, not just a broad interest

#### Paragraph 3: Connection to DoD Mission
- How does your research serve defense needs?
- Unmanned systems are a top DoD priority -- connect propeller acoustics to stealth UAV operations
- CFD for hypersonic/advanced vehicle design
- Flight dynamics for autonomous air vehicles
- Emphasize national security language (not environmental/civilian framing)

#### Paragraph 4: Specific DoD Facilities (Critical Section)
- Name 2-3 specific facilities and explain HOW your work fits their programs
- Show you researched what they actually do
- Express genuine desire to work there beyond the service obligation
- This is where most applications fail -- generic = rejected

#### Paragraph 5: Long-Term Career Goals
- Where do you see yourself in 10+ years in the DoD civilian workforce?
- Express commitment to continued service beyond the obligation
- Connect to broader career in defense aerospace R&D

### What NOT to Do
- Don't leave any application section blank (automatic zero points)
- Don't mention environmental causes unless directly tied to facility work
- Don't be vague about facilities -- "I'd love to work at any Air Force base" is a death sentence
- Don't just list achievements -- provide narrative context with quantifiable metrics
- Don't write generic STEM enthusiasm -- write to your future DoD mentor as the audience

## 6. DoD Facility Targeting -- Top 5 for Keelan

### Tier 1 (Best Fit -- List These)

#### 1. Air Force Research Laboratory -- Aerospace Systems Directorate (AFRL/RQ), Wright-Patterson AFB, OH
- **Why:** This is the single best match. AFRL/RQ does vehicle aerodynamics, flight control, aerospace propulsion, structures, turbine engines, hypersonics, and unmanned systems. They have 1,900+ people and extensive CFD capabilities. They overlay CFD with wind tunnel and flight test data.
- **Research fit:** Propeller aeroacoustics, CFD for unmanned vehicle design, propulsion optimization
- **Essay angle:** "AFRL/RQ's work on next-generation propulsion and autonomous aerospace vehicles directly aligns with my research in UAV propeller optimization. I'm particularly drawn to the directorate's integrated approach of coupling CFD analysis with flight test data..."
- **Location:** Dayton, OH area. Affordable cost of living.

#### 2. Naval Air Warfare Center Aircraft Division (NAWCAD), Patuxent River, MD
- **Why:** NAWCAD is the Navy's primary center for aircraft R&D, testing, and evaluation. They do extensive work on naval aircraft aerodynamics, propulsion, and unmanned systems (MQ-25 Stingray, MQ-8 Fire Scout, etc.).
- **Research fit:** UAV flight testing, propulsion systems, aeroacoustics for carrier-based unmanned systems
- **Essay angle:** "NAWCAD's role as the Navy's aircraft development and test center, particularly their work on unmanned carrier aviation..."
- **Location:** Southern Maryland. Naval aviation culture.

#### 3. Naval Air Warfare Center Weapons Division (NAWCWD), China Lake, CA
- **Why:** Weapons testing, UAS/UCAV development, propulsion systems, missile aerodynamics. State-of-the-art test ranges.
- **Research fit:** Unmanned combat air vehicle testing, propulsion, aerodynamic analysis
- **Essay angle:** Focus on their extensive test range infrastructure for validating CFD models against real flight data
- **Location:** Remote desert location in California. Unique test environment.

### Tier 2 (Strong Fit -- Consider)

#### 4. Air Force Research Laboratory -- Munitions Directorate (AFRL/RW), Eglin AFB, FL
- **Why:** Aerodynamic design and CFD for weapons/delivery systems. Strong computational capabilities.
- **Research fit:** CFD, aerodynamic optimization, flight dynamics of high-speed vehicles
- **Location:** Florida panhandle, good quality of life

#### 5. Army Combat Capabilities Development Command (DEVCOM) -- Aviation & Missile Center, Redstone Arsenal, AL
- **Why:** Army's primary center for rotorcraft, UAVs, and missile systems. Does propulsion, aerodynamics, and flight dynamics for Army aviation.
- **Research fit:** Rotorcraft/UAV aerodynamics, propulsion, acoustics (Army is heavily invested in Future Vertical Lift and UAS)
- **Location:** Huntsville, AL. Major aerospace hub. Keelan already knows Alabama.

### Facility Selection Strategy
- **Rank AFRL/RQ Wright-Patterson first.** It is the strongest match and one of the largest SMART sponsors.
- Facilities search the applicant database and prioritize candidates who listed them. Listing a facility first signals genuine interest.
- Research each facility's specific current programs before writing the essay
- Express desire to continue working at the facility beyond the service obligation
- Do NOT select "no preference" -- facilities interpret this negatively
