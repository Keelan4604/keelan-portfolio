### #1 PICK: Micro-SaaS — Contractor Job Tracker / Simple CRM (Score: 76)

**The Thesis:** Keelan is already talking to contractors every day through website sales outreach. Small contractors (1-5 person shops) track jobs on paper, text messages, and memory. They miss follow-ups, lose invoices, and can't tell you how much they made last quarter. The existing tools (Jobber, Housecall Pro, ServiceTitan) cost $49-$199/mo and are bloated for a one-truck operation.

**Demand Signal (20/25):**
- Indie Hackers data: 73% of successful micro-SaaS businesses target micro-segments that larger competitors ignore
- Micro-SaaS market growing at 30% annually ($15.7B to $59.6B by 2030)
- SBA stat: 89% of tradespeople lack adequate digital tools
- Every website sales call is a customer discovery call — Keelan can literally ask "what do you use to track jobs?" during outreach
- Revenue data from similar tools: $39-$99/month per user is the sweet spot

**Unit Economics (22/25):**
- Pricing: $29-$49/mo per user (undercut Jobber's $69/mo starting tier)
- COGS: Near zero (hosted on Cloudflare Workers + D1 database, or Supabase free tier)
- 50 users at $39/mo = $1,950/mo recurring
- 200 users at $39/mo = $7,800/mo recurring
- LTV is high because contractors are sticky once they have job history in your system
- Built-in distribution: every website sales prospect is a potential SaaS user

**Saturation (13/25):**
- Jobber, Housecall Pro, ServiceTitan, and GorillaDesk all exist and are well-funded
- BUT they are all $49-$199+/mo and overbuilt for solo operators
- The "too simple to exist" gap is real — no one has nailed a $29/mo "just track my jobs and send invoices" tool
- 70% of micro-SaaS businesses make under $1K/mo — so most attempts fail, but the winners win big

**Keelan's Leverage (21/25):**
- Already talking to the exact target customer through website sales
- Technical enough to build the MVP in a weekend (Python/JS + Supabase or Cloudflare)
- Can bundle it: "I built your website AND here's a tool to manage the leads it generates"
- Engineering background means the product will actually work well
- Can dogfood it himself for the website sales business

**Recommended Action:**
1. During every website sales outreach call, ask: "How do you currently track your jobs and invoices?"
2. Document the answers in a spreadsheet — this is free customer discovery
3. After 10-15 conversations, build an MVP with: job creation, status tracking, customer contact list, basic invoicing
4. Offer it free to first 5 website clients as a bundle bonus
5. Start charging $29-$39/mo after 3 months

**Speed to first dollar:** 4-8 weeks (after customer discovery during website sales calls)

## Key Patterns Noticed

1. **The "too cheap to exist" gap is real.** In every vertical researched (med spas, dental, contractors, wedding), there's a pricing gap between DIY ($0-$20/mo templates) and agency ($5K-$15K). Keelan's $500-$3,000 range hits this gap perfectly. The micro-SaaS version of this is the $29-$49/mo contractor tool vs. $69-$199/mo Jobber/Housecall Pro.

2. **Every outreach call is a customer discovery call.** The website sales pipeline isn't just a revenue channel — it's a free research channel. Every conversation with a contractor reveals their software pain points, job tracking habits, and willingness to pay for tools. This is the foundation for the micro-SaaS play.

3. **Ann Arbor is the geography upgrade.** Higher median income, tech-savvy population, independent business culture, and UMich's entrepreneurship ecosystem (free legal, accelerators, pitch competitions). The move from Tuscaloosa to Ann Arbor isn't just academic — it's a business upgrade.

4. **Recurring revenue beats everything.** One-time website sales at $500-$2,000 require constant hunting. The chatbot ($149-$249/mo), the contractor SaaS ($29-$49/mo), and the med spa automation ($200-$500/mo) are all recurring. 20 clients at $100/mo average = $2,000/mo that compounds. That's the real business.

5. **Med spa / dental is high-ticket but high-competition.** The budgets are real ($3K-$16K/mo marketing spend) but so are the competitors (12+ specialized agencies). This is worth pursuing opportunistically but shouldn't be the primary focus.
