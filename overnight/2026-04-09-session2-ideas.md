## Key Patterns Noticed

**Pattern 1: Session 1's data is Session 2's evidence.**
The lead research from Session 1 (37 documented businesses with specific problems) is the strongest demand evidence for 3 of the 4 top ideas. The portfolio/case study service, the audit service, and even bid writing all have direct evidence in those 37 profiles. Keelan doesn't need to build new pipelines — he has the targets.

**Pattern 2: Saturation kills the obvious AI agency plays.**
AI voice agents, review response automation, local SEO retainers — all are being taught in YouTube courses and sold by dozens of agencies. Any idea that has a Udemy course about it loses most of its saturation score. The highest-scoring ideas are the ones that require a specific context (engineering background, local Alabama knowledge, documented lead list) that most people can't replicate.

**Pattern 3: Low-friction entry products, then upsell.**
The $200-350 audit and the $400 portfolio build are more likely to get Keelan his first money than cold-pitching $2,000 website redesigns. The funnel is: audit → fix → ongoing retainer OR portfolio build → website redesign.

**Pattern 4: Engineering background is an underused asset.**
Two of the four top ideas score high specifically because Keelan is an aerospace engineering student. Most "AI agency" people are marketing-background folks without technical credibility. Keelan can go into domains (technical docs, bid specs, engineering-adjacent manufacturers) that they can't.

**Pattern 5: Physical-world presence beats remote competition.**
In Alabama, local presence with in-person meetings beats any Upwork freelancer or national agency for small contractors. Keelan can show up. Most competition can't (or won't).
