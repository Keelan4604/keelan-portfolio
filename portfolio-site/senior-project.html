Senior Design Case Study

Fixed-Wing eVTOL Platform for First-Responder Mission Profiles

This project focused on developing a fixed-wing electric VTOL aircraft capable of vertical takeoff, forward transition, and efficient cruise performance. The work combined systems engineering, CAD-driven integration, controls logic, and phased testing.

My Role and Ownership

Primary contribution: CAD architecture and full mechanical integration.

  • Owned large portions of the SolidWorks airframe model and subsystem packaging.
  • Designed around real dimensional constraints for propulsion, actuation, avionics, wiring, and payload volume.
  • Built a geometry workflow that supported rapid iteration between aerodynamic intent and manufacturable structure.
  • Helped align subsystem interfaces so control, power, and mechanical teams could converge on one build-ready configuration.

Beyond CAD, I also contributed to transition modeling support, stability criteria discussion, and technical review preparation.

Engineering Objectives

Configuration and Mission Fit

Deliver a compact fixed-wing VTOL layout that can lift vertically, transition safely, and cruise efficiently for practical operations.

Systems Integration

Create a design where propulsion, actuation, electronics, and payload interfaces are coordinated from the start rather than patched late.

Technical Workstream

1) CAD + Structural Packaging

I developed and iterated the integrated CAD model to enforce fit, routing, and access constraints while preserving aerodynamic continuity. Particular emphasis was placed on wing-body transitions, internal bay allocation, and component serviceability.

2) Transition and Controls Collaboration

The team used MATLAB-oriented modeling to evaluate tilt/transition behavior and actuation sequencing. I supported this by ensuring the physical geometry and actuator placements stayed consistent with simulation assumptions and practical control authority.

3) Verification Path

The validation plan used staged checks: bench-level subsystem checks, constrained hover/transition tests, then broader flight envelope confirmation for stability and responsiveness.

Design Decisions I Drove

  • Airframe layout discipline: Reduced interference among wiring runs, actuators, and propulsion components by locking interface zones early.
  • Integration-first CAD process: Prioritized subsystem compatibility before cosmetic surface finalization to minimize redesign cycles.
  • Modularity considerations: Supported a payload-forward architecture with defined mounting and access regions to allow future mission swaps.
  • Manufacturability awareness: Balanced geometric performance with realistic assembly constraints and iterative prototyping needs.

Results and Impact

The project produced a coherent fixed-wing eVTOL design framework with validated subsystem integration, transition-oriented controls work, and a practical test progression. My CAD-focused contributions significantly accelerated team convergence by turning concept-level intent into a physically consistent, build-ready architecture.
