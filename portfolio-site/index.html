About Me

Engineering the
future of flight

I grew up taking things apart to figure out how they worked -- RC planes, computers, anything with moving parts. That curiosity led me to aerospace engineering, where the stakes are higher and the physics is beautiful. I earned my BS in Aerospace Engineering from the University of Alabama, studying everything from supersonic flow to orbital transfers. Now I'm at the University of Michigan for my Master's, pushing deeper into computational methods and advanced aerospace systems.

I'm most at home at the intersection of physics and code. My work in CFD and finite element analysis is about turning real-world aerodynamic and structural problems into simulations that reveal better designs. I'm fluent in MATLAB, ANSYS Fluent, SolidWorks, and CATIA -- and I write the scripts to automate the analysis pipeline too.

Outside the lab, I build software and websites for local businesses. I believe the best engineers are the ones who can both design the hardware and write the code to analyze it -- and I'm driven to be one of them.
